## The short answer

1. **Every payment Capxul builds ends in one `PaymentSent`.** It carries the payment id, sender and recipient
   (indexed), then token, amount, kind and document hash (`v4/interfaces/IPayments.sol:23-31`). Budgets,
   Agreements and Escrow emit a sibling event in the same transaction that repeats the payment id and adds the
   budget, the scheduled payment, the agreement or the escrow. Nothing else about a payment is on chain.
2. **The document hash is the only field that can say what a payment is for.** It is one 32-byte word per payment,
   never zero (`v4/money/Payments.sol:56`), not indexed, and never read by any contract. It is hashed into the
   payment id (`Payments.sol:80-95`), so once the id is fixed the hash cannot change. Its meaning lives entirely off
   chain. Today the book hashes a Memo, a request's Invoice, an offer's terms, a payslip or a receipt file, each bound
   to one Payment (`books/target/documents/data.ts:76-90`). Nothing commits to "these lines of these invoices, for
   these amounts".
3. **The payment id** is `keccak256(abi.encode(TYPEHASH, chainid, Payments, jobNonce, executor, recipient, token,
   amount, kind, documentHash))` (`Payments.sol:15-17`, `:80-95`). Whoever builds the item chooses the job nonce.
   Budgets, Agreements, Rounds, Exits and AgentOwner derive theirs. The sender is not in the hash. `PaymentSent`
   carries neither the job nonce nor the executor, so only the side that prepared a payment can recompute its id.
4. **Money also moves with no `PaymentSent`.** This covers:
   - deposits, including Bridge's on-ramp, which is a plain ERC-20 Transfer into the Safe;
   - escrow funding from outside payers;
   - escrow refunds and surpluses;
   - a Board batch's direct token transfer, which the Gate does not refuse;
   - the company's other bank accounts, which have no chain at all.
5. **For an accounting system the events are naive in specific ways** (section 7). The ones that matter most:
   - a payment carries no allocation to documents or lines;
   - there is no partial payment, no amount outstanding and no reversal;
   - there is no fee, currency, value or tax;
   - no money event names the person or agent who acted;
   - the budget appears only on a sibling event;
   - a Bridge payout reads on chain as "paid Bridge";
   - `kind` is used inconsistently;
   - a payment to oneself reports an amount that never moved.

**The one-way rule, and its limit.** `pv4/architecture.md:135-137` says: "Every movement of a company's money is a
`Payments.send` or `Payments.sendBatch` with a nonzero document hash and a `kind`." That holds for every function
the protocol offers. The Gate is the Safe's only guard, and it refuses only these (`pv4/spec/board.md:51-53`):

- changes to owners, threshold, modules, guard or fallback handler;
- calls into the Safe itself;
- delegatecalls outside its allow-list.

So a Board transaction that calls `token.transfer` directly passes, and emits only the token's Transfer. This is
inferred from that list. The book catches such a transfer as a Movement (section 6).

## 2. How a payment id is formed, and who chooses it

`Payments._paymentId` (`Payments.sol:80-95`) hashes:

- the type hash `CapxulPayment(uint256 chainId,address router,bytes32 jobNonce,address executor,address recipient,address token,uint256 amount,uint16 kind,bytes32 documentHash)` (`:15-17`);
- `block.chainid` and `address(this)`;
- every item term except `paymentId` itself.

`send` recomputes the id and refuses a mismatch (`:59-60`). It refuses a set executor that is not the caller
(`:61-63`) and an id already paid (`:64`). Then it marks the id paid before moving the money (`:67`).

The sender is not hashed. The book notes this in its settlement rule (`books/target/payments/decisions.ts:319`).

| Path | Job nonce given to Payments | Who chooses it | Executor | `PaymentSent.sender` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Personal or Board `Payments.send` / `sendBatch` | the item's own | whoever builds the item (Capxul's prepare) | the builder's choice: zero lets anyone send the item; the book's Board route sets the company Safe (`books/target/payments/commands.ts:196`) | the calling Safe |
| `Budgets.spend` | `keccak256(abi.encode(Budgets, budgetId, callerNonce))` (`Budgets.sol:229`) | the hat wearer picks `callerNonce` (Capxul's prepare) | the company Safe (`Budgets.sol:515`) | the company Safe |
| `Budgets.schedule` then `execute` | the same derivation, from the nonce stored at schedule (`Budgets.sol:263`, `:296`), so the id is known before the schedule lands (`IBudgets.sol:245-247`) | the wearer, at schedule | the company Safe | the company Safe |
| `Agreements.collect` → `Budgets.payReserved` | `keccak256(abi.encode(Agreements, agreementId, collectionIndex))` (`Agreements.sol:219-222`), passed through unchanged (`Budgets.sol:338`) | nobody: derived from a counter | the company Safe | the company Safe |
| Escrow `payout` / `collect` | `terms.jobNonce` (`Escrow.sol:73`, `:145`) | whoever builds the terms | the escrow for a claim escrow, zero for an invoice escrow (`Escrow.sol:146`; `EscrowFactory.sol:46-60`) | the escrow |
| Rounds | `keccak256(abi.encode(noteId))` and the like (`Rounds.sol:114`, `:230`) | derived | Rounds (`Rounds.sol:382`) | Rounds, after it pulls the investor's tokens (`Rounds.sol:372`) |
| Exits `buy` | `keccak256(abi.encode(Exits, transferId))` (`Exits.sol:231-239`) | derived | Exits | Exits, after it pulls the buyer's tokens |
| AgentOwner sweep | `keccak256(abi.encode("capxul:agent-sweep", safe, count))`; its document hash is also synthetic (`AgentOwner.sol:356-365`) | derived | the agent's Safe | the agent's Safe |

What follows for identity:

- **Known before signing.** The id can be computed before anyone signs, from the terms and the nonce. The book shows
  it at prepare and keys everything on it (`books/target/payments/data.ts:141-150`).
- **One id, one payment.** An id pays at most once (invariant P3, `pv4/spec/payments.md:123`; `Payments.sol:64`).
  Sending the same terms with the same nonce twice is refused, which makes a retry idempotent.
- **A different nonce is a different payment.** The same terms paid with another nonce are a new payment, and the
  contracts cannot tell whether that is a double payment.
- **Hard to recompute from the log.** An observer that sees only the log cannot recompute the id: `PaymentSent` lacks
  the job nonce and the executor. For a budget spend, `Spent` lacks the caller's nonce. Only `PaymentScheduled`
  emits a nonce (`IBudgets.sol:80-90`).

## 3. Every money event, field by field

`(i)` marks an indexed field. Every log also carries its contract address, block, transaction and log index. The
block gives the time.

### Payments

**`PaymentSent(bytes32 (i) paymentId, address (i) sender, address (i) recipient, address token, uint256 amount, uint16 kind, bytes32 documentHash)`**
(`IPayments.sol:23-31`). It is emitted after the exact-transfer check (`Payments.sol:77`).

An indexer can learn:

- **Which payment:** the id, globally unique, because chain and contract are in the hash.
- **The hop's source and destination:** `sender` and `recipient`.
- **What moved:** the token and the raw amount, and that exactly that amount reached the recipient (`:68-75`).
- **The kind,** as a number.
- **One commitment,** the document hash.

It cannot learn:

- **The economic payer.** `sender` is whoever called Payments: an escrow, Rounds or Exits for the payments that pass
  through them.
- **The budget, department, agreement or escrow behind it.** Each is only on the sibling event.
- **The person or agent who caused it.**
- **The job nonce or executor,** so it cannot recompute the id.
- **The decimals, the currency, or any value.**
- **Any fee.** Payments has none, and it refuses fee-on-transfer tokens (`:73-75`).
- **What the document hash means.**
- **A batch or line number.**
- **Whether the payment is part of a larger amount, or refunds an earlier payment.**
- **Whether money moved at all, when `recipient == sender`.** In that case the required balance change is zero, yet
  the event still reports `amount` (`:71-75`).

Funding is never an escrow event:

- An invoice escrow fills from any number of Transfers from any wallet (`Escrow.sol:99-100`).
- A claim escrow is funded by a Transfer, or by a `PaymentSent` or `Spent` whose recipient is the escrow's address.

### The general escrow the book designs (not in v4's code)

`aaron.escrow-general` changes Escrow and EscrowFactory only (`docs/design/escrow.md:106-141`). The changes:

- The roles are renamed: `owner` collects and `sender` takes back.
- New terms: `authority` and `authorityKey`, a term for the bootstrap owner, and `ownerTakesSurplus`.
- A new view, `canRefund`.
- The events change:
  - `EscrowCreated(owner, sender, paymentId, token, amount, kind, documentHash, condition, authority, authorityKey)`;
  - `EscrowRefunded(sender, by, paymentId, amount, state)`, which adds who took the money back;
  - `EscrowDeployed(escrow, owner, sender, paymentId, salt)` (`docs/design/escrow.md:97-104`;
    `books/target/shared/external.ts:1383-1384`, `:1436-1449`).

What stays:

- A refund is still a plain transfer, with no `PaymentSent`.
- A budget's refund still restores no room (invariant G10, `docs/design/escrow.md:165`).
- `InvoicePaymentCompleted` is not booked (`books/target/shared/decisions.ts:2514`).

| Path | Logs, in order | Joins an indexer can make |
|---|---|---|
| Personal `payment send` | `Approval`; `Transfer` (payer Safe → payee); `PaymentSent` (sender: the payer Safe); `UserOperationEvent` | id ↔ payer Safe ↔ the person |
| Several items (`sendBatch`) | the same per item; one `PaymentSent` each, in item order | each line's id and log index; the batch is the transaction (no batch id; `Payments.sol:42-46`) |

| Payroll run | N times (`Transfer`, `PaymentSent`, `Spent`) in one operation | each slip's id; the run is the transaction |

| Bridge payout returned | `Transfer` (Bridge → source Safe) | matched by the refund transaction Bridge names (`banking/processes.ts:169-192`) |

## 5. The questions, answered

- **Which payment.**
  - **On chain:** `paymentId`, globally unique, consumed once, known before signing.
  - **In the book:** the key `payment:<id>` for both sides' book facts (`books/target/books/decisions.ts:134-163`).
- **Who paid, from which Safe.**
  - **On chain:** `PaymentSent.sender` is the address the tokens left in that hop. That is the payer's Safe or the
    company Safe for Capxul's own payments, but an escrow, Rounds or Exits for payments routed through them.
  - **Not on chain, in any money event:** the person or agent who acted. The only trace is the transaction's
    `UserOperationEvent`.
- **From which budget.**
  - **Only from a sibling event:** `Spent`, `ScheduledPaid` or `ReservedPaid` with the same `paymentId`.
  - **A Board payment and a personal payment have none.**
  - **The department's name** is only in Capxul's budgets records.
- **To whom.**
  - **On chain:** `PaymentSent.recipient`, that hop's destination.
  - **Escrow funding:** the recipient is the escrow address, and the real payee is in its terms (`EscrowCreated`).
  - **Bridge payouts:** the recipient is Bridge's deposit address. The bank payee is off chain.
- **How much of which token.** `token` and the raw `amount`, exact. There are no decimals, no currency and no value.
  `Budgets` amounts are `uint128` and Payments' are `uint256`.
- **Fees.**
  - **None on chain.** Payments requires exact delivery.
  - **Bridge's fees** are in the quote (`fee_amount`, kept as `fee_raw`; `banking/payouts.ts:84-86`) and in the
    deposit's gross less net (`banking/processes.ts:239`).
  - **Capxul's own Bridge fee** is a setting of the Bridge account (`developer_fee_percent`,
    `banking/data.ts:363`), inside that gross less net.
  - **Gas** is a paymaster's (`user-operations.paymaster`, `actual_gas_cost`).
- **A reference or memo.**
  - **On chain:** only the document hash, one per payment, with no plaintext.
  - **Many invoices:** it can name many only if the document it hashes lists them. No document kind the book has
    today does that (section 6).
- **A batch's per-line identity.**
  - **Each line:** its own `paymentId` and log index.
  - **No batch id:** there is no batch id, batch total, or line number.
  - **Atomic:** a batch is one atomic transaction.
- **Partial payments.**
  - **Not a concept.** Each payment is one exact amount, and no contract knows a total or what is outstanding.
  - **Instalments** are separate payments with different nonces. Nothing on chain links them unless their documents
    do.
  - **An invoice escrow** accumulates partial funding without events, then pays one amount.
- **Refunds and reversals.**
  - **No reversal.** Payments has no undo and no link from one payment to another.
  - **A refund** is either a new payment the other way, with nothing linking it, or a plain transfer (escrow
    refunds, Bridge returns).
  - **`ScheduleCancelled`** reverses a reservation, not a payment.
  - **A chain reorg** is the only true reversal. The book reopens and reverses on it (`payments/data.ts:445-450`).

## 6. What the book records about each money fact today

- **Payments' ledger** (`books/target/payments/data.ts:137-289`). One row per Payment, with:
  - **The chain terms:** `chain_payment_id`, `job_nonce`, `kind`, `payer_safe`, `payee_safe`, `token_address`,
    `asset`, `decimals`, `amount_raw`, `document_id` and `document_hash`.
  - **How it is carried:** `route`, `pay_at`, `budget_id`, `scheduled_id`, `escrow_address`, `funding_payment_id`.
  - **What it is for:** `against` and `payee_against` (account, party, cost centre, project, and `references` to
    documents with amounts allocated), `source` (a payroll slip, remittance, expense claim, advance, seat purchase
    or hosting), and `fee_raw`.
  - **The settled evidence:** transaction, log, block, block hash, block time.

  It settles only from the indexed `PaymentSent` with its id. The settle guard compares Payments' address, chain,
  sender, recipient, token, raw amount, kind and document hash (`:431-438`; decision `payments.one-settlement-path`,
  `payments/decisions.ts:300-330`). A `PaymentSent` that no Payment holds gives birth to one, whose origin is
  `chain` (`payments/processes.ts:221-235`). Its `against` is empty and its document is unknown, unless Capxul stored
  one.
- **Movements** (`payments/data.ts:293-341`). One row per side of a Transfer touching a Capxul Safe that no Payment
  explains. It is booked under `transfer:<chain>:<tx>:<log>`, with no reference of any kind.
- **Indexing:**
  - `chain-payments` (`books/target/indexing/data.ts:239-297`): every `PaymentSent`, with `budget_id`,
    `scheduled_id`, `agreement_id` and `escrow_address` filled from the sibling event in the same transaction.
  - `chain-budgets` (`:643-686`), `chain-agreements` (`:687-744`) and `escrows` (`:745-806`).
  - `user-operations` (`:298-344`).
  - The book's contract records omit `ReservedPaid`, `Configured`, `AgreementsSet` and `InvoicePaymentCompleted`
    (`books/target/shared/external.ts:1219-1343`). Collections join through `Collected`, which carries the payment
    id.

  The book overstates two things: it says "every movement of money in v4 ends in a PaymentSent"
  (`external.ts:1224-1225`; `indexing/data.ts:243`). Deposits, escrow refunds and direct Board transfers do not.
- **Books' facts** (`books/target/books/data.ts:546-652`; `books/target/books/procedures.ts:66-94`):
  - **One money fact per side,** keyed `payment:<id>` or `transfer:<chain>:<tx>:<log>`, with a generation.
  - **Kinds:** `payment_settled`, `payment_received`, `deposit_received`, `payment_sent_outside`, `payout_returned`,
    `opening_balance` and `reversed`.
  - **Contents:** amount, asset, Safe, counterparty and its Party, `fee_raw`, source, chain, block, transaction and
    log, and USD at the block, once.
  - **No account.** The voucher's other side is the row's annotation when it is read (`books.account-at-posting`).
- **Annotations** (`books/data.ts:421-468`). The annotation carries `references`: document type, name, and an
  allocated amount in the Company's currency (`:119-133`). It can be saved again at any time, and the last write
  wins.
- **Vouchers.** They are derived by the rules `frappe-v1`. A party account makes a Payment Entry allocated to the
  documents named, and anything else makes a Journal Entry "Bank Entry" with the transaction as cheque number
  (`books/decisions.ts:929-985`). ERPNext needs a reference number and date for any bank-type account
  (`/tmp/erpnext/erpnext/accounts/doctype/payment_entry/payment_entry.py:1450-1456`). A Payment Entry Reference names
  a whole document, an allocated amount and optionally a payment term, never a line (`payment_entry_reference.json`
  fields).
- **What reaches Frappe.**
  - **On Journal Entry and Payment Entry:** `capxul_id`, `capxul_version`, `capxul_tx_hash`, `capxul_chain_id`,
    `capxul_log_index` and `capxul_block_time` (`docs/design/frappe/accounting.md:365-368`;
    `books/target/books/frappe.ts:64-67`).
  - **Missing from vouchers:** the payment id and the document hash.
  - **Salary slips** do carry `capxul_chain_payment_id` and `capxul_document_hash`
    (`books/target/payroll/decisions.ts:1256`).
- **Documents** (`books/target/documents/data.ts:29-31`, `:76-90`; `documents/procedures.ts:36-63`, `:146-160`):
  - **Five kinds:** memo, invoice, terms, payslip and receipt.
  - **Document hash:** EIP-712 over the typed message. It is bound to its chain by the domain and to its subject by an
    origin id, `keccak256(subject kind, id)`. So "one note on two Payments is two documents".
  - **Invoice:** the book's Invoice is a request's, which Capxul issues. It has 1 to 100 lines of description,
    quantity and unit amount, and no tax.
  - **`payment send`:** it hashes the note's Memo or a receipt file (`payments/commands.ts:68-69`, `:114`). A
    receipt's document hash is its bytes' hash.
  - **No kind lists several documents with amounts.** Paying a supplier's invoice that lives in Frappe therefore
    commits on chain to a Memo, not to that invoice.
- **Bridge payout.** One Payment to Bridge's deposit address, with:
  - kind 0;
  - document hash `keccak256(payout id)`, with no stored document;
  - `fee_raw` from the quote;
  - `against` the contact as Supplier (`banking/payouts.ts:69-90`; `banking/decisions.ts:917-945`).

  Delivery and return belong to banking's payout machine, not to payments.
- **Bridge deposit.** No Payment. Banking credits it on the exact Transfer of the net amount and writes
  `deposit_received`, keyed by the Transfer, with `fee_raw` as gross less net (`banking/processes.ts:236-241`). It
  is matched to a request by hand with `bank match` (`books/target/requests/deposits.ts:1-4`).
- **Escrow.** Nothing posts while money waits. The voucher posts at collection, and a refund cancels the Payment and
  posts nothing (`books.escrow-held`, `books/decisions.ts:1427-1455`).

## 7. Where the events are naive for an accounting system

1. **One opaque reference, unindexed and unchecked.**
   - **The facts:** `documentHash` is a data field (`IPayments.sol:30`). No log filter can find "every payment of
     document X". No contract checks what it means.
   - **Today's binding:** the book binds each document to one Payment (`documents/data.ts:87`). Three instalments of
     one invoice therefore carry three unrelated hashes.
2. **No allocation.**
   - **The gap:** a payment cannot say on chain which documents, lines or tax rows it pays, or how much to each.
   - **Where the meaning lives:** Frappe's `references` come from the annotation, which can be edited after
     settlement. Nothing proves a Payment Entry's allocation is what the payer committed to.
3. **The id cannot be recomputed from the log.**
   - **Missing fields:** the job nonce and the executor are not emitted. For a budget spend, the caller's nonce is
     not emitted either (`Budgets.sol:229-231`).
   - **Consequence:** a verifier on Frappe's side cannot check from the chain alone that an id binds a given
     document. It needs the item from Capxul.
4. **`sender` is a hop, not the payer.**
   - **Who the sender is:** an escrow's, Rounds' or Exits' address for payments routed through them (`Escrow.sol:149`;
     `Rounds.sol:372-386`; `Exits.sol:244-250`).
   - **Not bound:** the sender is not in the id, so an item with no executor can be paid by anyone (`Escrow.sol:146`,
     `:124-130`).
5. **No actor.**
   - **What is missing:** no money event names who acted for a department (`Budgets.sol:231`).
   - **`execute`:** anyone may call it (`:283`).
   - **Attribution** needs the transaction's `UserOperationEvent` and Hats' state at that block.
6. **The budget is beside the payment, not on it.**
   - **Where it is:** only `Spent`, `ScheduledPaid` and `ReservedPaid` name it.
   - **Board payments** have no budget.
   - **`BudgetCreated` has no name.**
   - **Agreements' pay** never counts in a budget's `spent` (`Budgets.sol:337-340`), so the chain's spent figure
     understates a department's cost.
7. **Batches have no structure.** `sendBatch` emits one event per item, with no batch id, total or line number
   (`Payments.sol:42-46`). A payroll run is N spends in one operation.
8. **No partial payments or outstanding amounts.** Each payment is exact, and the contracts know no invoice total.
   An invoice escrow's partial funding is just Transfers (`Escrow.sol:99-100`).
9. **No reversal.**
   - **What exists:** Payments has no undo and no link to an earlier payment.
   - **Plain transfers with no `PaymentSent`:** escrow refunds (`Escrow.sol:183-185`) and Bridge returns.
   - **`ScheduleCancelled` has no amount** (`IBudgets.sol:91`).
   - **A budget's escrow refund** restores no room (invariant G10).
10. **No fee.** Payments refuses fee-on-transfer tokens (`Payments.sol:73-75`). Bridge's fees and Capxul's fee are
    off chain (`banking/data.ts:363`, `:886`).
11. **`kind` is coarse, unindexed and used inconsistently.**
    - **v4's table:** 2 is payroll and 1 is invoice (`pv4/architecture.md:151-165`).
    - **What the book pays:** salary slips go as "kind one-off" (`payroll/staged.ts:75`), every `payment send` as kind
      0 (`payments/commands.ts:117`), and a Bridge payout as kind 0.
    - **Budgets' limit:** spends are limited to kinds 0 to 3 (`Budgets.sol:448-450`).
    - **The book's own rule:** never classify by kind (`docs/design/frappe/accounting.md:450`, C26).
12. **No currency, value or tax.** Amounts are raw token units. Books prices them in USD at the block
    (`books/data.ts:546-560`), and tax exists only in Frappe's documents.
13. **Only block time.**
    - **Missing dates:** there is no invoice date, value date or due date. `payAt` exists only for schedules.
    - **Two calendars:** budget periods are UTC calendar periods (`pv4/architecture.md:167-169`), while the Company's
      fiscal calendar and time zone are Frappe's.
14. **A payment to oneself lies.** When `recipient == sender`, `PaymentSent` reports `amount` while no balance moves
    (`Payments.sol:71-75`). A spend from a budget to the company Safe itself would do this.
15. **Money with no `PaymentSent`.** This covers:
    - deposits, including Bridge's on-ramp;
    - escrow funding from outside;
    - refunds and surplus;
    - direct Board transfers;
    - other bank accounts, which are not on chain at all.

    The book books Safe-touching ones as Movements, with no reference (`payments/data.ts:293-303`).
16. **A Bridge payout reads as "paid Bridge".**
    - **The recipient** is Bridge's deposit address.
    - **The document hash** is `keccak256(payout id)` and names no document (`banking/decisions.ts:917-945`).
    - **Off chain:** the fiat payee, the invoice and the fee.
17. **Agreements have no accrual and no period on a collection.**
    - **The amount** is the payee's choice, up to what is released (`Agreements.sol:216-217`).
    - **The document hash** is the same terms hash for every collection (`:222`).
    - **The collection index** is hidden in the nonce.
    - **So** a collection cannot say "March's salary".
18. **One economic payment can be two payments.**
    - **The two:** escrow funding (Safe → escrow) and payout (escrow → payee) are two `PaymentSent`s with different
      ids. The payee appears only in the escrow's terms.
    - **What is missing:** v4's `EscrowCreated` lacks the job nonce, and `EscrowPaid` lacks the surplus.
19. **Events the book does not read.** `ReservedPaid`, `InvoicePaymentCompleted`, `Configured` and `AgreementsSet`
    (`external.ts:1219-1343`; `shared/decisions.ts:2514`). Only `InvoicePaymentCompleted` carries money information
    nothing else gives: the surplus returned.
20. **The chain identity never reaches Frappe's vouchers.** Payment Entry and Journal Entry get the transaction, log,
    chain and block time, but not the payment id or document hash (`accounting.md:365-368`).

## 8. What can be built without a contract change, and what cannot (input for the design)

Every payment path already takes a nonzero document hash, and the id binds it. So the document hash can commit to a
richer document with no contract change. Its fields would be:

- each allocation: document type, number and content hash, the lines or tax rows paid, and the amount;
- payer, payee, token and amount;
- for a refund, the payment it refunds.

The payment id then commits to that allocation. The document can be checked by hashing what Frappe's Payment Entry
references and comparing.

**What this fixes:**

- items 2 and 8, by putting the allocation inside each payment's document;
- part of 9, because a refund's document can name what it refunds;
- 16, because a payout can hash the invoice it pays instead of `keccak256(payout id)`;
- 20, which is a Capxul and Frappe field, not a contract field;
- 1 for Capxul, since its indexer reads every log anyway.

**What only a contract change fixes:**

- **17:** a per-collection document. `collect` stamps the terms hash (`Agreements.sol:222`), so a collection's
  meaning must stay in Capxul's records, keyed by payment id.
- **The refund part of 9:** a refund that passes through Payments, linked to the payment it returns.
- **14:** the self-payment amount.
- **3 for outside verifiers:** emitting the job nonce and the executor.
- **5:** an actor on `Spent`.
- **6:** a budget on `PaymentSent`.

Items 5 and 6 can already be joined from the same transaction.

**Not a chain matter at all:**

- 10 (fees), 12 (currency, value and tax) and 13 (dates);
- deposits' payer identity, which Bridge's events give;
- money in other bank accounts, which is booked and reconciled in Frappe as usual.
