## What
Fit the loop's command policy (packages/loop/src/modules/capxul/internal/config.ts, POLICY and READS) to the harmless reads and checks agents asked for and were refused on 11 November, and keep every refusal that protects something. Allow: `sed -n` with a /pattern/ or /pattern/,/pattern/ address (no `e`, `w` or `s` command); `bash -n <files>` (syntax check only); `git ls-tree`, `git check-ignore`; `test`/`[` and `which`; `pnpm plan show` (read only; every other plan subcommand stays refused); `pnpm portal` and `pnpm portal:check` (they build into the lane); a command wrapped in a subshell `( ... )` when what is inside is allowed. Keep refused, with their hints: driving the loop (`pnpm loop ...`), paths outside the lane, git that moves branches or bypasses hooks (`git -c core.hooksPath=...`), a reader committing, python (use node), network. Each change with a test in the policy's tests: one allowed and one still refused per line. Then the Runs page (packages/loop-ui/src/app/data.ts and where it is shown): say "N of M calls blocked by the rules" in grey, not amber, since a refusal stops a command, not the run; a run with refusals is not unhealthy by that alone.
## Why
Aaron, 11 November: "refused by the loop policy" shows up all the time and he asked why. Of today's refusals most were harmless reads the policy did not know (sed with a pattern, bash -n, git ls-tree), so agents lost turns; a few were right (an agent starting the loop host, a reviewer committing, a hook bypass).
## Done when
- pnpm loop:test passes, with an allowed and a still-refused test for each policy change
- The Runs page shows refusals as "blocked by the rules", not as a failure
